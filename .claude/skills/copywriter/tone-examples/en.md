# English tone — professional companies

## Hero examples by sector

### B2B SaaS

**Headline:** Close the books in two days, not two weeks.
**Subhead:** Accounting, tax, and reporting in one place. Free for 14 days.
**Primary CTA:** Start free
**Secondary CTA:** Book a demo

### Agency / professional services

**Headline:** We build brands that get bought, not just liked.
**Subhead:** Strategy, identity, and launch for B2B companies $5M–$50M.
**Primary CTA:** Book an intro call

### Manufacturing / industrial B2B

**Headline:** Custom metal fabrication, on time since 2008.
**Subhead:** 1,200+ projects delivered. ISO 9001. In-house design, machining, and install.
**Primary CTA:** Request a quote
**Secondary CTA:** See past projects

### E-commerce / DTC

**Headline:** Picked for you. Returns are free.
**Subhead:** Order today, get it tomorrow.
**Primary CTA:** Shop now

### Healthcare / professional services (consumer)

**Headline:** Dentistry, without the dread.
**Subhead:** 15+ year doctors, digital scans, no-interest payment plans.
**Primary CTA:** Book a visit

## Feature block — benefit-led examples

- "Close month-end in 2 days" ✓ (bad: "Faster month-end" ✗)
- "Same-day delivery in Tashkent" ✓ (bad: "Fast shipping" ✗)
- "Your own account manager — replies in Slack within 10 minutes" ✓ (bad: "Dedicated support" ✗)
- "5-year warranty, written in the contract" ✓ (bad: "Reliable quality" ✗)

## Tone observations by category

- **B2B SaaS:** confident, specific, slightly understated. The trend over the last five years is shorter, plainer copy — Linear, Vercel, Stripe set the register. Avoid hype.
- **Agency / luxury:** more white space, fewer feature lists, sentence-fragment headlines that imply rather than explain. Premium brands say less.
- **DTC / e-commerce:** warmer, second-person ("you"), contractions ("we're", "you'll"), light personality. Can be playful if the brand is.
- **Industrial / manufacturing:** specific and proof-heavy. Years in business, certifications, project counts, named clients. Skeptical buyers respond to numbers, not adjectives.
- **Healthcare / regulated services:** calm and clear, not salesy. Reassurance through specifics (years of experience, technology, payment options), not through hype.

## Words and phrases to avoid

- "Unlock" — anything
- "Leverage" — as a verb
- "Seamless"
- "Delve into"
- "Revolutionize" / "transform" / "elevate" / "empower"
- "Cutting-edge" / "state-of-the-art" / "best-in-class" / "world-class"
- "Journey" (as in "your X journey")
- "Robust" / "scalable" / "powerful" without something concrete attached
- "Innovative" / "innovation" (almost always filler)
- "Holistic" / "ecosystem" / "synergy"
- "Welcome to [company name]" as a hero — never works
- "Your trusted partner in X" — empty trust claim

## Patterns that work

- **"X without Y":** "Project management without the meetings." "Hiring without the spam."
- **Specific number + time:** "Save 4 hours a week." "Ship 3x more features."
- **Pivot from the expected category answer:** "Not another CRM. A CRM that gets used."
- **Plain division of labor:** "You ship the feature. We handle the rest."
- **Permission-giving subhead:** Lead with a bold claim; let the subhead say who it's actually for.
- **Sentence fragments for premium feel:** "Built for builders." "For founders who've seen it work."

## CTA examples

**Good:**
- Start free
- Book a demo
- Get a quote
- Talk to sales
- See pricing
- Try the demo

**Empty (avoid):**
- Submit
- Click here
- Learn more (a link label, not a CTA)
- Get started (overused — be specific: "Start free" / "Create your first project")
- Contact us (replace with the channel: "Email sales" / "Chat with us")

## Risk-reversal lines (near final CTA)

- "No credit card required."
- "Free for 14 days. Cancel anytime."
- "30-day money-back guarantee."
- "Free consultation. No obligation."
- "Talk to a human, not a chatbot."

## Voice & register notes

- **Default to American business English** unless the user signals UK, AU, or international. Don't mix US/UK spellings on the same page.
- **Contractions are fine in B2B SaaS and DTC.** Skip them in legal, finance, and luxury.
- **Second person ("you") is the default subject.** First-person plural ("we") only when describing what the company specifically does.
- **Title case vs. sentence case:** sentence case is the modern default for most categories. Title case still fits law/finance/luxury.
- **Em dashes are fine. Semicolons usually aren't** — they signal academic register and slow the reader down.
